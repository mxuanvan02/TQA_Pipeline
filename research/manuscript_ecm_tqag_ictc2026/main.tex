\begin{abstract}
Attaching a page image to a question-generation prompt does not make the resulting
item multimodal. We formalise this gap for textbook question answering (TQA) and
close it from two sides. First, we replace the visual \emph{condition label} with an
executable \emph{cross-modal contract}: the planner must request a motif whose
template declares a visual step, and that step must bind a declared page image,
commit to a normalised bounding box, and report an observation, or the construction
is refused by local code before any question is worded. Second, because a
construction-side contract can still yield a question answerable from prose alone,
we add a falsifiable acceptance criterion: a paired with-image/without-image
ablation, run by an answerer from a third model family on the frozen item, defines
the visual-dependence rate $\vdrate$. On 16 OCR-derived chunks from 9 Vietnamese
law textbooks with a manifest-pinned image alignment, restricting the visual
condition to cross-modal motifs raises its contract pass rate from 2/16 to 8/16
under an otherwise identical generator, seed and manifest (paired McNemar,
7 versus 1 discordant chunks, $p=0.070$), and eliminates ungrounded-anchor and
missing-visual-binding rejections. The ablation then falsifies the stronger reading
of that gain: of the 8 contract-passing visual items, only 1 requires the image
($\vdrate=0.125$, Wilson 95\% CI $[0.02,0.47]$), 6 are text-sufficient, 1 is broken
by the image, and mean answerer accuracy is identical with and without the image
($0.875$ versus $0.875$). We report this negative result as the primary empirical
finding: structural cross-modal guards are necessary but demonstrably insufficient,
and $\vdrate$ supplies the missing falsifiable test that pass rates cannot provide.
\end{abstract}

multimodal question generation, textbook question answering, visual dependence, evidence-grounded generation, provenance-aware generation, falsifiable evaluation

\section{Introduction}
Textbooks distribute instructional content across prose, headings, tables,
diagrams, charts, and page layout. A multimodal textbook question-answering (TQA)
item therefore benefits from an inspectable relation among its answer, supporting
evidence, and visual or structural context. Document models combine text,
geometry, and visual representations \cite{xu2020layoutlm,xu2021layoutlmv2,huang2022layoutlmv3};
document, table, and chart QA demonstrate the role of structured and visual
evidence \cite{mathew2021docvqa,herzig2020tapas,tito2023multipage,kafle2018dvqa,masry2022chartqa};
and question generation supports visual and educational authoring
\cite{mostafazadeh2016generating,zhu2016visual7w,heilman2010good,kurdi2020systematic}.

This paper starts from a failure we observed in our own pipeline. When the visual
condition was implemented as an \emph{evidence label} --- text plus layout plus an
attached page image --- the generator was free to request a text-only reasoning
motif, satisfy every structural check using prose anchors, and emit an item that
was called multimodal because an image had been supplied. The image was present in
the prompt and absent from the derivation. That construction is unfalsifiable: no
artefact of the item records whether the image mattered.

We treat this as a specification problem rather than a prompt-tuning problem, and
we address it at two distinct levels that are easy to conflate.

\emph{Construction level.} Multimodality becomes a mechanism. The motif catalog is
partitioned into text-only and cross-modal templates; a cross-modal template
declares a step with \texttt{modality} $=$ \emph{visual}. Under the visual
condition the planner may request only cross-modal motifs, and a declared visual
step is compiled only if it names a declared image, commits to a normalised
bounding box, and reports an observation. A visual anchor is deliberately never
scored against prose, so it can neither be satisfied by paraphrasing text nor
counted toward the text-side distinctness floor.

\emph{Item level.} A contract cannot certify necessity. A question may be built
through an image and still be answerable from the prose alone. We therefore define
the visual-dependence rate $\vdrate$ from a paired ablation on the frozen item:
an independent answerer, from a model family disjoint from the generator, answers
the identical question and choices with and without the page image; the item is
visual-dependent only if the answerer is correct with the image and incorrect
without it.

The contributions are: (i) an executable cross-modal contract that makes the
visual pathway a compile-time requirement rather than a prompt-level suggestion
(Section~\ref{sec:protocol}, Algorithm~\ref{alg:construct});
(ii) $\vdrate$, a falsifiable, replayable acceptance criterion for visual
necessity that is independent of the construction checks
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:vd}); (iii) a paired measurement of what the contract buys on
the construction side (Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}); and (iv) the negative result
that the contract does \emph{not} buy visual necessity
(Section~\ref{sec:vdresults}), with a diagnosis of why.

\subsection{Input and multimodal evidence representation}
The user-facing input is an OCR-derived chunk from a textbook PDF. The chunk
supplies extracted text and may retain source location metadata; the system
associates structural cues and the corresponding page visual when the selected
condition permits them. We represent the available evidence as
$D=(T,L,V,M,\Pi)$: text, structure/layout, visual regions, metadata, and
source-level provenance. Parsing and alignment produce
\begin{equation}
G=(N,E,\rho),\qquad N=N_T\cup N_L\cup N_V\cup N_M,
\end{equation}
where $\rho:N\rightarrow\Pi$ links each node to a source descriptor such as page,
region, character span, and extraction metadata. This source mapping is distinct
from the item-level trace $\Gamma$ \cite{moreau2013prov} and is recomputed
deterministically from the supplied chunk.

For each selected chunk, let $c\in K=\{\T,\TL,\TLV\}$ denote an evidence
condition. ECM-TQAG forms the paired evidence packages
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{X}_{\T}=(T),\quad \mathcal{X}_{\TL}=(T,L),\quad \mathcal{X}_{\TLV}=(T,L,V).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Motif catalog and the cross-modal contract}
The catalog $\mathcal{M}$ is closed and contains $|\mathcal{M}|=7$ templates. Each
template fixes an ordered step list; a step declares whether it needs an anchor and,
if so, its modality. Let
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M}_{V}=\{m\in\mathcal{M}: \exists\, s\in m,\ \mathrm{mod}(s)=\text{visual}\}
\end{equation}
be the cross-modal subset, with $|\mathcal{M}_V|=2$
(\texttt{diagram\_text\_reconcile}, \texttt{figure\_condition\_apply}). The
admissible catalog is condition-dependent:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}(c)=
\begin{cases}
\mathcal{M}_V, & c=\TLV,\\
\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathcal{M}_V, & c\in\{\T,\TL\}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:admissible}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:admissible} is the mechanism. Offering text-only motifs under
$\TLV$ is precisely what allowed the visual condition to degenerate into
``$\TL$ plus a label''; offering cross-modal motifs under $\T$ or $\TL$ would
guarantee a compile rejection, since no image is declared.

A declared visual step $s$ compiles only if all of the following hold, where
$\mathcal{V}$ is the declared image list of the package:
\begin{align}
&\mathrm{node}(s)\in\{1,\dots,|\mathcal{V}|\}, \label{eq:vc1}\\
&b(s)=(x_0,y_0,x_1,y_1)\in[0,1]^4,\ x_0<x_1,\ y_0<y_1, \label{eq:vc2}\\
&\mathrm{obs}(s)\neq\varnothing. \label{eq:vc3}
\end{align}
Conditions~\eqref{eq:vc1}--\eqref{eq:vc3} are checked by local code, not by the
model. A visual anchor is excluded from text matching by construction: scoring it
against prose would mark every cross-modal step ungrounded, which is exactly the
behaviour of a text-only executor. Consequently the text-side distinctness floor
is computed over text-anchor steps only,
\begin{equation}
\phi(m)=\min\bigl(\mathrm{minAnchors}(m),\,|\{s\in m:\mathrm{mod}(s)=\text{text}\}|\bigr),
\end{equation}
so a cross-modal motif is neither privileged nor penalised relative to a text-only
motif on the text side.

\subsection{Evidence-first construction}
Construction follows one spine,
\begin{equation}
\text{OCR chunk} \rightarrow G \rightarrow G_m \rightarrow z \rightarrow (A,\Gamma) \rightarrow q \rightarrow \mathcal{C},
\end{equation}
with $A=\mathrm{Exec}(z,G_m)$ and $\Gamma=\mathrm{Trace}(z,G_m)$. Algorithm~\ref{alg:construct}
states the procedure; every \textbf{reject} is a terminal, recorded outcome rather
than a retry, so a rejected construction is never silently converted into a valid
item.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{ECM-TQAG construction with the cross-modal contract}
\label{alg:construct}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require package $\mathcal{X}_c$ with text $T$, structure $L$, images $\mathcal{V}$; condition $c$; catalog $\mathcal{M}$
\Ensure item $(q,\mathcal{O},y,\Gamma)$ or a typed rejection
\State $\mathcal{A}\gets\mathcal{A}(c)$ \Comment{Eq.~\eqref{eq:admissible}}
\State $P\gets\textsc{Planner}(\mathcal{X}_c,\mathcal{A})$ \Comment{stage 1: sees evidence}
\If{$P$ is not valid JSON} \State \Return \textbf{reject} \textsc{invalid\_json} \EndIf
\If{$\mathrm{motif}(P)\notin\mathcal{A}$} \State \Return \textbf{reject} \textsc{motif\_not\_admissible} \EndIf
\State $m\gets\mathcal{M}[\mathrm{motif}(P)]$
\If{$|\mathrm{steps}(P)|\neq|m|$} \State \Return \textbf{reject} \textsc{step\_count\_mismatch} \EndIf
\ForAll{$s\in\mathrm{steps}(P)$ with $\mathrm{mod}(s)=\text{visual}$}
  \If{Eq.~\eqref{eq:vc1}, \eqref{eq:vc2} or \eqref{eq:vc3} fails}
    \State \Return \textbf{reject} \textsc{no\_visual\_binding}
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\If{$c=\TLV$ and $m\notin\mathcal{M}_V$} \State \Return \textbf{reject} \textsc{visual\_contract} \EndIf
\State $z\gets\textsc{Compile}(m,P)$; $\ \Sigma\gets\textsc{Sentences}(T)$
\State $u\gets0$; $\ \mathcal{S}\gets\varnothing$
\ForAll{$s\in\mathrm{steps}(z)$ needing an anchor}
  \If{$\mathrm{mod}(s)=\text{visual}$}
    \State mark $s$ \textsc{visual}; \textbf{continue} \Comment{never scored on prose}
  \EndIf
  \State $(i,\sigma)\gets\arg\max_{j}\ \mathrm{idf\text{-}overlap}(\mathrm{anchor}(s),\Sigma_j)$
  \If{$\sigma\ge\tau$} \State $\mathcal{S}\gets\mathcal{S}\cup\{i\}$ \Else{} \State $u\gets u+1$ \EndIf
\EndFor
\If{$u>0$} \State \Return \textbf{reject} \textsc{ungrounded\_steps}$(u)$ \EndIf
\If{$|\mathcal{S}|<\phi(m)$} \State \Return \textbf{reject} \textsc{anchors\_not\_distinct} \EndIf
\State $(A,\Gamma)\gets(\mathrm{Exec}(z,G_m),\mathrm{Trace}(z,G_m))$
\State $\hat{z}\gets\textsc{Seal}(A,\Gamma)$ \Comment{no chunk text, no image}
\State $(q,\mathcal{O},y)\gets\textsc{Realizer}(\hat{z})$ \Comment{stage 2: blind to source}
\If{$\mathcal{C}(q,\mathcal{O},y,A,\Gamma)$ fails} \State \Return \textbf{reject} \textsc{check\_set} \EndIf
\State \Return $(q,\mathcal{O},y,\Gamma)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The realizer receives only the sealed construction $\hat{z}$: no chunk text and no
page image. This separation is what makes the anti-verbatim and leakage checks
meaningful, and it also means the realizer cannot recover visual information that
the planner failed to record.

\subsection{Validation and replay}
The check set $\mathcal{C}$ enforces a well-formed derivation, non-empty answer
atoms, atom--trace correspondence, four distinct options, an in-range answer index,
answer--choice agreement, a maximum verbatim run, and an excerpt leakage bound.
Replay recomputes the source mapping, motif match, compiled program, answer atoms
and trace from the manifest without network access. These checks establish
structural and provenance validity only; semantic correctness, legal correctness,
pedagogical quality and visual necessity require separate assessment.

\section{A Falsifiable Criterion for Visual Necessity}
\label{sec:vd}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:construct} certifies that the \emph{planner} used the image. It
says nothing about the \emph{question}. We therefore define an item-level criterion
that is independent of every construction check.

Fix a contract-passing item with question $q$, ordered choices $\mathcal{O}$, gold
index $y$, evidence text $T$ and page image $v$. Define two arms that differ in
exactly one factor,
\begin{equation}
\pi_{+}=(q,\mathcal{O},T,L,v),\qquad \pi_{-}=(q,\mathcal{O},T,L),
\end{equation}
and require byte-identical $q$, $\mathcal{O}$ and $T$ across arms; a fingerprint
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{fp}=\mathrm{SHA256}\bigl(\mathrm{canon}(q,\mathcal{O},T)\bigr)
\end{equation}
is recomputed per trial and a mismatch aborts scoring rather than producing a
plausible-looking number. An answerer $\mathcal{J}$ from a family disjoint from the
generator returns only $\{\texttt{answer\_index}\}$; it never sees $y$, the
rationale, the motif, the bounding box, or the condition label. With $k$ repeats
per arm and majority correctness $\mu_{\pm}\in\{\text{true},\text{false},\perp\}$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{verdict}=
\begin{cases}
\textsc{indeterminate}, & \mu_{+}=\perp \text{ or } \mu_{-}=\perp,\\
\textsc{unanswerable}, & \mu_{+}=\text{false},\\
\textsc{text\_sufficient}, & \mu_{+}\wedge\mu_{-},\\
\textsc{visual\_dependent}, & \mu_{+}\wedge\neg\mu_{-},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:verdict}
\end{equation}
and the headline quantity is
\begin{equation}
\vdrate=\frac{|\{\text{items}:\textsc{visual\_dependent}\}|}{|\{\text{contract-passing items tested}\}|}.
\label{eq:vdr}
\end{equation}

Three design choices in Eq.~\eqref{eq:verdict} are deliberate. \textsc{unanswerable}
is separated from \textsc{visual\_dependent} because an item that is wrong even
\emph{with} the image is defective, and counting it as evidence about vision would
be an artefact. Repeats with a strict majority prevent a single flipped trial at
temperature $0$ from promoting an item, and \textsc{indeterminate} keeps unstable
items out of the numerator instead of rounding them in. Finally, the denominator in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:vdr} is every contract-passing item submitted, not only the items that
survive; otherwise $\vdrate$ would be $1$ by construction and would claim nothing.
Chance accuracy $1/|\mathcal{O}|=0.25$ is reported alongside, since a guessable
option set can be answered without either modality.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Visual-dependence ablation (defines $\vdrate$)}
\label{alg:vd}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require contract-passing items $\mathcal{I}$; answerer $\mathcal{J}$ (family $\neq$ generator); repeats $k$
\Ensure verdict per item and $\vdrate$
\State $\mathcal{R}\gets\varnothing$
\ForAll{$(q,\mathcal{O},y,T,L,v)\in\mathcal{I}$}
  \If{$v=\varnothing$} \State skip and record \textsc{no\_image}; \textbf{continue} \EndIf
  \State $\mathrm{fp}\gets\mathrm{SHA256}(\mathrm{canon}(q,\mathcal{O},T))$
  \ForAll{$\mathrm{arm}\in\{+,-\}$, $r\in\{1,\dots,k\}$}
    \State $\hat{y}\gets\mathcal{J}(\pi_{\mathrm{arm}})$ \Comment{returns only \texttt{answer\_index}}
    \State record $(\mathrm{fp},\mathrm{arm},r,\hat{y},[\hat{y}=y])$
  \EndFor
  \State \textbf{assert} one $\mathrm{fp}$, both arms present, image present iff $\mathrm{arm}=+$
  \State $\mu_{+},\mu_{-}\gets$ majority correctness per arm (failed trials excluded)
  \State $\mathcal{R}\gets\mathcal{R}\cup\{\text{verdict by Eq.~\eqref{eq:verdict}}\}$
\EndFor
\State \Return $\mathcal{R}$, $\vdrate$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:vdr}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

No step of Algorithm~\ref{alg:vd} recomputes the item, so every verdict is
replayable from the recorded trial ledger without network access.

\label{sec:setup}

image--page verification and package construction are recorded in a versioned
manifest of 48 evidence packages and 18 verified page images; every request is
keyed to the manifest digest \texttt{dac6ba98\ldots}, and each attached image is
re-hashed at call time, so a cell can be replayed against the exact bytes it
consumed. Crossing 16 chunks with 3 evidence conditions gives the 48-cell

\section{Does the Cross-Modal Contract Change Construction?}
\label{sec:mechanism}
We first measure what Eq.~\eqref{eq:admissible} buys, holding the generator, seed,
manifest, prompts and guard thresholds fixed and manipulating only the catalog
exposed under $\TLV$. The pre-contract run offered all text-only motifs under
$\TLV$; the post-contract run restricts $\TLV$ to $\mathcal{M}_V$. Pairing is by
chunk, so each chunk contributes one before/after pair
(Table~\ref{tab:mechanism}).

The contract quadruples the $\TLV$ pass rate, from 2/16 to 8/16, with 7 chunks
gained and 1 lost. The exact McNemar test on those 8 discordant chunks gives
$p=0.070$, so at $n=16$ the improvement is a consistent directional effect that
this sample does not establish at the $0.05$ level. The mechanistic evidence is
stronger than the $p$-value alone. The two rejection families that the contract
targets both collapse: \textsc{ungrounded\_steps} falls from 9 to 1 and
\textsc{no\_visual\_binding} from 4 to 0. Under the pre-contract catalog, the
generator chose a text-only motif under $\TLV$ (for example
\texttt{hierarchy\_traverse}), earned no exemption for any step, and then failed
text-anchor matching. That failure mode is removed by construction, not by
loosening a threshold: no guard parameter differs between the two runs.

The gain is not free. Rejections shift into \textsc{invalid\_json}, from 0 to 6:
emitting a cross-modal plan with a bounding box is a harder formatting task, and
the current runner retries transport failures only, never contract failures. Six
of the eight post-contract $\TLV$ rejections are therefore formatting faults rather
than reasoning faults, which bounds how much of the remaining headroom is about
multimodal competence at all.

contract removes the degeneracy of the visual condition; we do not claim that the
visual condition is easier or harder to satisfy than the text conditions.

\section{Does the Contract Deliver Visual Necessity?}
\label{sec:vdresults}
All 8 contract-passing $\TLV$ items bind a declared image, commit a bounding box
satisfying Eq.~\eqref{eq:vc2}, and report an observation. By every
construction-side check they are multimodal. Algorithm~\ref{alg:vd} tests whether
they are, at the item level, with 48 answerer calls and no failed trials
(Table~\ref{tab:vd}).

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Visual-dependence ablation over the 8 contract-passing $\TLV$ items
(\texttt{gemini-2.5-flash}, $k=3$ repeats per arm, 48 calls, 0 failed trials).}
\label{tab:vd}
\small
\resizebox{\columnwidth}{!}{%
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\hline
Verdict (Eq.~\eqref{eq:verdict}) & Items & Share \\
\hline
\textsc{text\_sufficient} & 6 & 0.750 \\
\textsc{unanswerable} & 1 & 0.125 \\
\textsc{visual\_dependent} & 1 & 0.125 \\
\textsc{indeterminate} & 0 & 0.000 \\
\hline
$\vdrate$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:vdr}) & \multicolumn{2}{c}{$0.125$, Wilson 95\% CI $[0.022,0.471]$} \\
Mean accuracy with image & \multicolumn{2}{c}{$0.875$} \\
Mean accuracy without image & \multicolumn{2}{c}{$0.875$} \\
Paired with vs without image & \multicolumn{2}{c}{1 vs 1 discordant, $p=1.000$} \\
$\vdrate$ vs chance $0.25$ & \multicolumn{2}{c}{$p=0.689$} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}

\end{table}

The result is negative and unambiguous in direction. Only 1 of 8 items requires the
image. Six are text-sufficient: the answerer is correct in all three repeats of
both arms, so the image is decorative with respect to answering. Mean accuracy is
identical with and without the image ($0.875$ versus $0.875$), and the paired test
over items is exactly null (1 versus 1 discordant, $p=1.000$). The interval
$[0.022,0.471]$ contains the chance rate $0.25$, and $\vdrate$ does not differ from
chance ($p=0.689$), so this sample cannot even establish that $\vdrate$ exceeds
guessing.

One item is \textsc{unanswerable}: the answerer is wrong in all three with-image
repeats and correct in all three without-image repeats. Attaching the page image
actively degraded answering. Separating this verdict from
\textsc{visual\_dependent} matters --- a criterion that only asked ``did accuracy
change?'' would have counted this item as evidence for visual dependence when it is
evidence of a defective item.

Taken with Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}, the two experiments answer two different
questions and give opposite verdicts. The contract demonstrably changes
construction: the planner now must read the image, and the rejection families prove
it. The contract does not change the item: 8/8 pass the cross-modal guards while
1/8 needs the image. Structural cross-modal checks are necessary but not sufficient,
and no pass-rate statistic could have revealed this, because pass rate is computed
from the same contract it would have to falsify.

A diagnostic observation supports the same conclusion from a third direction. In a
pilot of three $\TLV$ cells on unrelated documents --- a world map and a civil
procedure flowchart --- two items emitted the identical bounding box
$[0.12,0.34,0.48,0.52]$. Eq.~\eqref{eq:vc2} validates format, not correctness of
localisation, so a habitual box passes. Bounding-box verification against the
rendered region, rather than against a format predicate, is the concrete next step
this finding identifies.

\section{Supporting Rubric Study}
\label{sec:judge}
A separate rubric study on an earlier generation matrix provides two facts that
bear on the interpretation above and are reported for completeness rather than as
evidence about the contract, which post-dates it. Two blinded judges from
disjoint families (\texttt{claude-sonnet-5}, \texttt{gpt-5.6-terra}) scored items
on faithfulness, answerability, distractor quality and reasoning depth under choice
permutation and condition blinding. Quadratic-weighted $\kappa$ ranged from
$0.369$ (distractor quality) to $0.686$ (answerability), i.e.\ moderate agreement,
substantial only for answerability; agreement within one scale point exceeded
$0.95$ on all four dimensions. Rubric scores are therefore reported as descriptive
agreement, not as semantic or legal validity. Second, absolute levels were far more
informative than contrasts: faithfulness and answerability sat near the top of the
scale while reasoning depth sat near the bottom, and the only dimension with a
significant Friedman result was depth ($p=0.024$), whose pairwise contrasts did not
survive Holm correction. Grounding was not the binding constraint; depth was. The
present paper adds that visual necessity is a second binding constraint, and unlike
depth it is not visible to a rubric at all --- a text-sufficient item can score
maximally on all four dimensions.

\section{Limitations}
Six limitations bound these results. First, scale: 16 chunks and 8 ablation items
give low power, and the absence of significance in
Tables~\ref{tab:mechanism}--\ref{tab:vd} is not evidence of equivalence; the
mechanism effect at $p=0.070$ and $\vdrate$ at $[0.022,0.471]$ both need a larger
corpus. Second, $\vdrate$ is model-based: it operationalises visual necessity for
one answerer from one family, and a stronger or weaker answerer would move it in
ways this design cannot separate from item properties. No human or expert anchor is
included. Third, the ablation removes the image entirely, so it measures necessity
of the page image as a whole and not of the specific region named by the bounding
box; a region-level ablation would be a sharper test. Fourth, the 6
\textsc{invalid\_json} rejections under the contract mean the post-contract pass
rate underestimates achievable performance for formatting reasons unrelated to
multimodal competence. Fifth, bounding boxes are validated for format only, and the
repeated-box observation shows this admits habitual output; we report it as a
diagnosis, not as a quantified error rate. Sixth, results are specific to one
open-weight vision--language generator, page-level image granularity, Vietnamese
legal textbooks, and the prompts used here.

\section{Conclusion}
We argued that a visual \emph{condition label} does not make a textbook question
multimodal, and we replaced it with two artefacts. On the construction side, an
executable cross-modal contract makes a visual anchor a compile-time requirement:
under the visual condition only cross-modal motifs are admissible, and a visual
step must bind a declared image, a normalised bounding box and an observation. This
raised the visual-condition pass rate from 2/16 to 8/16 and collapsed the
ungrounded-anchor and missing-visual-binding rejection families, with all other
factors held fixed. On the item side, the visual-dependence rate $\vdrate$ supplies
the falsifiable test that construction checks structurally cannot provide, and it
returned a negative verdict on our own pipeline: 8/8 items satisfy the cross-modal
guards, 1/8 needs the image, and answerer accuracy is identical with and without
it. We report that negative result as the paper's primary empirical finding.
Structural guards are necessary; only an ablation can certify necessity. The next
steps this identifies are concrete: verify bounding boxes against rendered regions
rather than a format predicate, ablate at region rather than page granularity, and
select for $\vdrate$ during construction instead of measuring it afterwards.
