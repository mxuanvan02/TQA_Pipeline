\begin{abstract}
ECM-TQAG is an evidence-first protocol for constructing traceable multimodal textbook question-answering items. It compares direct, answer-first, and evidence-chain generation from frozen text--structure--image packages. In the evidence-chain method, a planner first selects source-bound evidence units, their roles, a required-unit chain, and answer atoms. A separate realizer receives only this locked plan---not the original text or image pixels---and constructs one four-option question. Deterministic checks bind units to the frozen package, require trace and anchor coverage, and require a visual unit for a text--layout--vision plan. We release a reproducible runner, audit, tests, and a reporting design that separates structural/provenance validity from semantic correctness, pedagogical quality, and genuine visual necessity.
\end{abstract}
\begin{IEEEkeywords}
multimodal question generation, textbook question answering, evidence-grounded generation, provenance-aware generation, document understanding
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Textbooks distribute instructional content across prose, headings, tables, diagrams, charts, and page layout. A multimodal textbook question-answering (TQA) item must retain an inspectable relation among its answer and supporting evidence; attaching an image alone does not make an item visually grounded. Document models combine text, geometry, and visual representations \cite{xu2020layoutlm,xu2021layoutlmv2,huang2022layoutlmv3}; document, table, and chart QA demonstrate the role of structured and visual evidence \cite{mathew2021docvqa,herzig2020tapas,tito2023multipage,kafle2018dvqa,masry2022chartqa}; and question generation supports visual and educational authoring \cite{mostafazadeh2016generating,zhu2016visual7w,heilman2010good,kurdi2020systematic}. Yet a generation pipeline for source documents must preserve answer provenance before writing a question and assess whether visual pixels are actually needed.

This work addresses traceable multimodal TQA construction from textbook evidence. ECM-TQAG implements the following evidence-first sequence:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{X}_c \rightarrow (U,P,A) \rightarrow q \rightarrow \Gamma \rightarrow \mathcal{C},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{X}_c$ is a frozen package under condition $c$, $U$ is a set of source-bound evidence units, $P$ is a locked plan, $A$ is a list of answer atoms, $q$ is a four-option MCQ, $\Gamma$ is its unit-level trace and anchors, and $\mathcal{C}$ is a deterministic contract. The design follows compositional reasoning work on scenes, diagrams, and structured visual evidence \cite{johnson2017clevr,hudson2019gqa,kafle2018dvqa,masry2022chartqa} and provenance models that distinguish source records from derived artifacts \cite{moreau2013prov}.

The contributions are: (i) a frozen evidence-package representation with source and image-integrity receipts; (ii) a two-stage evidence-chain implementation that locks evidence units, roles, chain, and answer atoms before question realization; and (iii) an executable contract for source binding, plan--realizer consistency, trace coverage, and MCQ structure. ECM-TQAG is a construction protocol, not a document encoder or an answering model.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Frozen evidence packages}
For each selected textbook chunk, the pipeline builds exactly three immutable evidence packages:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{X}_{\T}=(T),\quad \mathcal{X}_{\TL}=(T,L),\quad \mathcal{X}_{\TLV}=(T,L,V),
\end{equation}
where $T$ is extracted text, $L$ is declared document structure, and $V$ is verified image pixels. The manifest records the chunk identity, source digest, and, when applicable, image byte size, digest, and declared order. The runner verifies this information before a request. The conditions are matched evidence settings: each is crossed with each generation protocol. Thus a parsed record establishes only that a specific package and protocol satisfied a structural/provenance contract; it does not establish item quality or visual grounding.

\subsection{Direct and answer-first baselines}
\emph{Direct} requests one four-option MCQ from a directly supported proposition. \emph{Answer-first} first requires a supported answer and then asks for a question and same-domain distractors; a deterministic check requires the declared answer to equal the selected option. Both protocols retain source-labelled evidence anchors, but neither is required to make a multi-unit chain.

\subsection{Evidence-Chain Method}
ECM uses two isolated model calls. The planner sees the selected frozen package and returns a plan
\begin{equation}
P=(U,R,H,A),
\end{equation}
where each $u\in U$ has an ID, modality, role, and content; $R\subseteq U$ is the required-unit set; $H$ is a sequence of chain steps from required IDs to intermediate conclusions; and $A$ contains answer atoms that occur as chain conclusions. A unit role is one of \emph{premise}, \emph{mapping}, or \emph{constraint}; at least two distinct roles are required. Text units must occur literally in $T$, structure units must equal the declared section title, and image units must identify an attached image by declared order.

The realizer receives only canonicalized $P$, not the original package or image pixels. It produces an MCQ, selected answer index, rationale, trace, and anchors. Each anchor must reproduce the ID, modality, and content of one required locked unit; each required unit must appear once in the trace. The realizer must preserve the ordered answer-atom list exactly. This separation prevents the realizer from silently substituting unplanned source evidence after planning.

For an ECM--$\TLV$ plan, at least one required unit must be visual and the planner must declare it necessary. This is a construction constraint, not proof that image pixels are semantically necessary for a human respondent. Visual necessity is evaluated separately by blinded review, consistent with work cautioning against language priors in visual QA \cite{goyal2017making,agrawal2018don}.

\subsection{Construction checks and provenance}
The check set $\mathcal{C}$ rejects malformed JSON, duplicate choices, invalid answer indexes, unbound unit IDs, non-literal text/structure units, missing chain coverage, changed answer atoms, incomplete traces, and anchors that differ from a locked unit. SHA-256 receipts bind the manifest and request inputs to each ledger record. These checks complement MCQ item-writing criteria \cite{haladyna2002review} and reproducibility/audit practices \cite{sandve2013ten,raji2020closing}; they do not establish legal correctness, unique-best-answer validity, distractor quality, difficulty, or image-grounding truth.

\section{Experiment and Reporting Design}
\label{sec:experiments}
The executable matrix contains eight selected chunks from five Vietnamese law textbooks, three evidence conditions, and three protocols: $8\times3\times3=72$ output cells. Direct and Answer-first make one request per cell. ECM makes a planner call and, only after a valid plan, a realization call; a complete run therefore plans 96 API calls. Results are written to a new, non-overwriting JSONL ledger with raw provider responses, prompt hashes, input-field receipts, plan/realizer provenance, and status records for parsed, rejected, and transport-error cells.

The present manuscript specifies the v3 evidence-chain protocol and reporting boundary. It intentionally does not reuse numerical results from earlier single-call or historical ledgers, because their contracts differ from the locked-planner/realizer implementation described above. Numerical generation, audit, and quality results will be reported only from a completed v3 ledger and its corresponding deterministic audit.

A post-generation quality gate has two levels. First, the mechanical audit checks record shape, distinct options, answer/choice agreement where applicable, source binding, unit roles, chain and atom consistency, trace coverage, locked anchors, and the ECM--$\TLV$ visual-unit requirement. Second, independent human or expert review must assess semantic correctness, one-best-answer validity, evidence sufficiency, distractor quality, pedagogical usefulness, and whether image pixels are genuinely needed. Automated parsing or contract validity is not a substitute for this review.

\section{Limitations}
The selected corpus establishes a reproducible feasibility setting, not generalization across document genres. The current contract does not prove that a chain is logically sound, that a legal statement is correct, that distractors are educationally appropriate, or that the image is counterfactually necessary. Those claims require documented expert review and learner-oriented studies \cite{kurdi2020systematic,haladyna2002review}. The visual requirement in the planner also cannot replace explicit visual-dependence evaluation \cite{goyal2017making,agrawal2018don}.

\section{Conclusion}
ECM-TQAG provides an executable evidence-first protocol for multimodal textbook MCQ construction. Its planner locks source-bound units, roles, chain, and answer atoms; its realizer receives only that plan; and deterministic checks preserve the resulting provenance record. The method is designed to make generation and auditing reproducible while keeping stronger claims about semantics, pedagogy, and visual necessity contingent on a completed quality evaluation.
